This study investigates how multimodal LLMs influence learner agency in experimental learning environments where attention demands are high and dynamic pedagogical adaptation is critical, addressing the research gap of multimodal interactions beyond conventional text interfaces.
workshop, two hands-on tasks
01

Research question

How does multimodal AI implicitly subvert or support student agency in experiential learning, and through what strategies of question formulation can students systematically improve their own agency?

RQ1 What makes a student question agentic in multimodal AI supported experimental learning?
02

Method

Workshop
Three stage structure
Each participant completed a pre task questionnaire on background and AI familiarity, two hands on electronic tasks, and a post task semi structured interview.
Experimental tasks
Participants constructed a basic LED circuit with calculations, followed by building and programming an Arduino traffic light system to combine conceptual reasoning with practical debugging.
Interaction protocol
Gemini Live served as the mandatory primary source of help, with external resources or human assistance permitted only after two failed attempts to track breakdown and recovery trajectories.
Multimodal data collection
Sessions captured real time problem solving through mobile screen recordings, behavioral observation, and audio recorded adaptive interviews probing interaction friction, workflow shifts, and agency fluctuations.
03

Data analysis

This study combined two analytical approaches to examine learner agency in a multimodal AI supported experimental learning environment. The first approach was bottom-up. Exploratory interviews were used to identify the situations in which participants felt multimodal AI supported or weakened their sense of agency during hands-on tasks. The second approach was top-down. A content analysis of 576 interaction questions was conducted using Bandura's theory of human agency, which identifies intentionality, forethought, self-reactiveness, and self-reflectiveness as core agentic properties (Bandura, 2006). The two approaches were then cross-checked against each other through case level comparison of individual learners, linking quantitative question profiles with the motivations participants described in interviews.

04

Result

Dual Impact: Multimodal LLMs enhances agency when offering low-pressure hints, conceptual explanations, and cross-modal troubleshooting (text, code, physical circuits). Conversely, it undermines agency through premature interruptions, rigid pacing, and inaccurate yet confident responses.

Core Mechanism: Question formulation serves as the primary hinge of learner control. Rather than being predetermined by prior subject expertise or overarching learning strategies, agency is dynamically enacted through the qualitative precision of learners' questions, marked by high self-reflectiveness (seeking principles) and self-reactiveness (troubleshooting feedback) alongside lower forethought and initial intentionality.

Table 2. Interview codes describing AI behaviors that influence learner agency.
Giving hints instead of complete answers
AI supports learning by giving cues, reminders, or directional prompts rather than taking over the whole task.
“I need AI to help me extract key points or give reminders, rather than directly taking over.”
Providing choices and processes instead of step-by-step guidance
AI enhances agency when it offers options, possible causes, or a process the learner can judge among.
“I wouldn't ask it what I should do; I would ask what the possibilities are, and then make a judgment.”
Providing more ways to solve problems (e.g., videos, Reddit)
Agency is supported when learners can draw on multiple resources beyond AI; it is reduced when AI becomes the only route.
“I usually look up Xiaohongshu, YouTube, and online tutorials to help me solve this problem, because AI cannot give me graphic tutorials.”
Helping beginner learners ask better questions through background knowledge, vocabulary, and structure
AI enhances agency when it provides beginner-friendly background knowledge, vocabulary, and structure that help learners formulate better questions.
“To tell the truth, I didn't even know what the English word for resistor was.”
Clear questions make it easier for AI to follow the learner's study plan
AI is more useful when the learner gives clear context, background, and precise wording.
“A very difficult point is that every time you ask it a question, before that you need to make sure it understands the background of your question.”
Using AI only for asking questions instead of letting it lead everything
Learners preserve agency by using AI as a tool for questioning, checking, or prompting rather than handing over the whole task.
“I need AI to help me extract key points or give reminders, rather than directly taking over.”
Letting AI know the learner's current stage
AI works better when it knows the learner's prior knowledge, current progress, and where they are stuck.
“Before it starts, it should first assess the user… ask about the user's knowledge base.”
Telling AI the learning goal: understanding each knowledge point or simply finishing the task
AI support should differ depending on whether the learner wants deep understanding, modeling, or quick completion.
“To complete this task, if you really want to understand it, you need knowledge of physical circuits.”
Emotional support and immediate responses
AI can enhance agency by being emotionally low-risk and readily available, so learners can ask repeatedly without embarrassment.
“When asking questions, there is no emotional pressure with it. You can control it infinitely and even scold it.”
06

Conclusion

Learner agency in multimodal AI supported experimental environments is not an intrinsic trait predetermined by prior expertise or general learning strategies, but an interactive practice dynamically governed by the quality of question formulation. While multimodal AI offers powerful cross-modal scaffolding and low-pressure conceptual support, its rapid pacing and overconfident inaccuracies can easily displace learner control if inquiries simply outsource procedural execution. Ultimately, sustaining agency requires students to use deliberate, situated questioning that prioritizes conceptual sensemaking, while future educational AI systems must be designed to scaffold reflection and adaptive pacing rather than merely delivering immediate answers.
